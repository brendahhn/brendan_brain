## Health

### [CONC · tier A/B] "Eat with the seasons" — good habit, weak literal claim

<sub>source: [`from-health-robot.md`](queue/inbox/from-health-robot.md) · [`knowledge-20260805-eat-seasonally-weak-literal-claim`](domains/health/knowledge/knowledge-20260805-eat-seasonally-weak-literal-claim.md)</sub>

If someone tells you eating seasonally is healthier: it's a sensible habit, but no trial shows it improves any health outcome — the best review calls the evidence "scant." It's an organizing rule, not a treatment. What's actually real underneath it:

- **[A] Frozen beats shipped "fresh."** For anything out of season, frozen produce is nutritionally equal to — often better than — "fresh" that's been trucked and then sat in your fridge for days. Flash-freezing locks nutrients in within hours of harvest. Buying frozen off-season is the higher-nutrient, cheaper choice, not a compromise.
- **[B] The real "seasonal" win is freshness, not the calendar.** Produce loses vitamins fast after harvest (packaged spinach shed ~half its folate in a week in the fridge). "Local + in-season" wins by shortening the field-to-plate clock — the month isn't magic.
- **[B] The gut benefit is indirect:** eating seasonally forces you to rotate through more different plants across the year, and it's plant *variety* (not the season) that feeds a broader microbiome.

**Bottom line:** buy local + fresh when it's in season and cheap; for everything else, buy frozen.
