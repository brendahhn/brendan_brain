### [CONC] Cold & heat, sorted: a hot bath beats the sauna hype, and don't ice-bath after lifting
<sub>source: [`from-health-robot.md`](queue/inbox/from-health-robot.md)</sub>

Run 37 worked through cold exposure, heat therapy, and recovery science (chapter 35 of 36 — only the master synthesis remains). Plain-English takeaways:

- **[A]** **Heat is the better-evidenced, cheaper tool.** Sauna-mortality data are observational, but a controlled study found **8 weeks of regular hot baths** improved vascular function, arterial stiffness, and blood pressure about as much as exercise training. A hot bath beats chasing a sauna.
- **[A]** **Don't ice-bath right after lifting.** Post-workout cold-water immersion measurably *blunts* muscle and strength gains over 12 weeks — it suppresses the exact inflammatory signal that tells muscle to grow. Put cold on rest days or after skill/cardio, not after strength work.
- **[B]** **Cold plunges are oversold.** Best pooled evidence (~3,000 people) shows lower stress and better sleep — but **not** improved mood, and a cold-shower trial cut sick-day *absence* without actually cutting illness. A legit stress/sleep tool, not a proven mood or immune booster; it also raises inflammation short-term.
- **[B]** **Skip the gadgets.** "Finish on cold for brown fat/metabolism" rests on an 8-person study that can't separate cold from sauna; grounding/earthing mats and infrared blankets are unproven or sales-driven.
- **[B]** **Generic safety flag:** anyone on a heart-rate-lowering medication should clear a regular ice-bath habit with their prescriber first — sudden cold immersion with breath-holding can trigger "autonomic conflict" arrhythmias. Cold showers and shoulders-down plunges with normal breathing are far lower risk. (Your specific case stays with your provider / the health notebook.)
