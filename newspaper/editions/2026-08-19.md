## Health

**This week's evidence review: what actually makes labor & delivery easier — and what's overhyped.** A careful, well-sourced pass separating the levers that hold up in the trials from the popular ones that don't. General evidence, not medical advice — anything here is a conversation for the pregnant person and their own OB/midwife.

- **[A] The single best-evidenced, no-cost, no-drug lever is continuous one-to-one support during labor.** A large Cochrane review (27 trials, ~15,800 women) found it raises the chance of a spontaneous vaginal birth and lowers cesarean, instrumental delivery, and epidural use — with shorter labor and no harms. The effect is **biggest when the supporter is a trained doula**; a present partner still adds real value on top.
- **[A] Two more free, well-evidenced levers:** staying **upright/mobile** (walking, birth ball) in early labor shortens the first stage by ~1.4 hours vs lying down; and **regular pregnancy exercise** (once cleared) lowers the odds of an instrumental delivery, with no added harm.
- **[B] Prep levers with solid evidence:** antenatal perineal massage from ~34–35 weeks reduces tearing/episiotomy (mainly for a first baby); second-stage warm compresses may reduce severe tears; first-stage water immersion lowers epidural use.
- **[B] Belief-corrections worth knowing:** the "natural induction" supplements — **evening primrose oil and raspberry-leaf tea — do NOT hold up** in the trials (no cervical-ripening benefit; some EPO safety flags). Eating **dates** late in pregnancy modestly improves cervical readiness but does **not** lower cesarean rates. And in a genuinely low-risk labor, **routine continuous fetal monitoring raises the cesarean rate without improving the baby's survival** vs intermittent listening — worth knowing when building a birth plan. _(General evidence, not medical advice; detail kept in the health repo.)_
