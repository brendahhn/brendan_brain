## Health  _(plain-English, general conclusions only)_

The health desk built the **real-time stress / nervous-system toolkit** you asked for — consolidating your loose "stress tips" into an evidence-ranked set. The organizing rule: **use the body to steady the mind first, then the reframe.**

### [CONC] Breathe first — a specific breath, not a thought
The fastest reliable in-the-moment tool is a **double-inhale through the nose, then a long exhale** (the "physiological sigh"). It beat other breathing styles and meditation head-to-head in a randomized trial. Do it first when you feel activated. (evidence tier A)

### [CONC] Then reframe nerves as excitement
Telling yourself the racing heart is your body *getting you ready* — rather than forcing calm — produced a healthier cardiovascular response and better performance across several trials. Sequence it: breathe, then reframe. (A)

### [CONC] "Stress is enhancing" is real but small
Viewing stress as helpful is a trainable belief that nudges your stress-hormone response healthier — but the overall effect of mindset techniques is **small** in a 2024 meta-analysis. A pre-installed edge, not a rescue button. (B)

### [CONC] Correction: the eye-movement trick is mis-stated
"Move your eyes side to side to switch off fear before speaking" is wrong as usually told. That EMDR-style move reduces the **vividness of a distressing image you're recalling** (do it beforehand, on the specific worry) — it's not an in-the-moment fear switch. (B)

### [CONC] Correction: power-breathing is a threshold practice, not a calm-down
Intense Wim-Hof-style breathing (rapid breaths + breath-holds) is a big **adrenaline surge** — a threshold-builder, not a calming tool, and its anti-inflammation result comes from a healthy-volunteer lab model, not a gut-disease study. **Safety note:** breath-holds carry a cardiac caution for anyone on a heart-rate-lowering med — gentle slow breathing gives the calm without the risk. (B)
