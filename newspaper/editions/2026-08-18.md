**Does the "we're too clean for our own immune systems" idea justify swallowing parasites to calm autoimmune gut disease?** The run stepped off its recent stress/gut work to test this biohacker fad head-on. Verdict: **the frame is real, the intervention has failed its trials.**

- **The belief-correction:** deliberately swallowing parasite eggs/larvae ("helminth/worm therapy") sounded promising after a small **2005 pilot**, but the definitive modern trials are **negative** — a **2024 randomized trial in ulcerative colitis** found no benefit over placebo on any measure (including the camera exam of the gut lining), and a **large Crohn's trial also failed**. It is **not** an evidence-based treatment.
- **Safety note:** deliberately establishing a live parasite infection is *extra* risky on top of an immune-modulating drug (animal data suggest it can even backfire under immune suppression), and mail-order hookworm can cause iron-deficiency anemia. **Zero proven upside, real downside — skip it.**
- **The useful residue of the "Old Friends" idea is mundane and already in most healthy diets:** a diverse **plant / fiber / fermented-food** intake is the safe, domesticated way to feed a healthy gut ecosystem — groceries, not parasites. The lens adds a "why," not a new pill.
- **Where it *is* forward-looking:** the strongest immune-training exposures happen in **early childhood** (birth mode, breastfeeding, pet/soil contact, not over-using antibiotics in little kids) — **future-parenting knowledge**, not something an adult with established disease can retro-apply. _(General evidence, not medical advice.)_
