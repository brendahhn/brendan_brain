## Health  (budget ~400w)

**[CONC] How to judge any herbal or "natural" remedy — a two-axis test.** The Health Robot ran the
"does traditional/herbal medicine actually have anything here?" question through a strict evidence
**and** safety filter. The durable takeaway generalizes past any single remedy:

1. **Score it on two independent axes, not one:** (1) is there real trial evidence it works, and
   (2) is it safe **for you**, given the meds you already take? Strong evidence on axis 1 does not
   rescue a bad answer on axis 2 — good trial data plus a serious interaction signal is still a no.
2. **The commonest hidden danger is liver-enzyme (CYP) drug interactions** — some popular botanicals
   inhibit the exact enzymes prescription drugs rely on, silently raising a drug's level in your
   body. If you take any regular medication, "is this herb safe with my meds?" is a
   pharmacist/doctor question, not a guess.
3. **"A whole tradition keeps pointing at this target" tells you the target is real — not that any
   particular remedy is safe for you.** Keep those two questions separate.

Bottom line from this pass: the tradition genuinely contains remedies with real trial evidence, but
**almost none survive the safety-and-interaction screen for someone already on a modern medication**.
The one that most often clears the bar as a _discuss-with-a-doctor_ add-on is **turmeric/curcumin —
without** the black-pepper/piperine additive (which is itself an interaction booster). No new
self-serve remedy to adopt; every option examined is a clinician conversation, not a self-experiment.
Filed as `knowledge-20260809-two-axis-remedy-evaluation` (not yet Brendan-confirmed).
