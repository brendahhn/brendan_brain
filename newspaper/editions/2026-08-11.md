## Health  (budget ~400w)

**[CONC] When a risky remedy proves a body target is reachable, find the FOOD that reaches it — not
the pill.** A direct follow-on to last week's two-axis remedy filter. The move that generalizes:

1. **Reachability is the transferable finding, not the remedy.** A strong-but-unsafe supplement is
   useful mainly as proof that a pathway responds to intake at all. Once that's established, the next
   question isn't "how do I get that remedy safely" — it's **"what ordinary food or habit reaches the
   same target?"** Often the answer is a vegetable you already know you should eat.
2. **The concrete lever here:** **cruciferous vegetables** (broccoli, cabbage, Brussels sprouts, kale,
   cauliflower) **plus a well-fed gut** (fiber, fermented foods, enough protein) feed a real
   diet→immune→gut-lining signaling system — a good, low-cost reason to keep them a standing habit.
   Honest caveat: the strongest evidence is still **lab/animal work**, so treat it as a sensible
   habit, **not a cure**.
3. **The same-compound-different-form trap:** the concentrated **supplement** of a vegetable's active
   compound (here, **"DIM"/I3C capsules**) can behave completely differently from the food — this one
   nudges liver enzymes in a way that can **lower the blood level of medications**. The vegetable's
   dose is negligible; the pill is a genuine interaction risk. **Eat the food; skip the concentrated
   capsule if you take any regular medication** (a pharmacist question). And two compounds from the
   *same* vegetable can hit different receptors and even work against each other — another argument
   for the whole food over an isolated high-dose pill.

Research-only pass; nothing to approve, no med change implied. Filed as
`knowledge-20260810-food-reaches-the-target-not-the-pill` (not yet Brendan-confirmed).
