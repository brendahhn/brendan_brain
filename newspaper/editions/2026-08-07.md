**Today — the "cost-per-day eating" project shipped** (closes the `eat-cheap-and-clean-meal-cost`
batch task). Verdict, plain-English:

- **[CONC] Clean and cheap are the same grocery list.** The cheapest protein-per-dollar foods —
  dried beans/lentils, eggs, whole tofu, canned wild salmon, rotisserie/thigh chicken, plain Greek
  yogurt — are also the least processed. Bars, RTD shakes, and deli meats lose on **both** cost and
  cleanliness; swapping them for eggs + a yogurt-or-tofu base + canned fish cuts ~50–70% per gram
  of protein and drops the additives in one move.
- **[CONC] Soy is fine as *whole* soy** (tofu, tempeh, edamame, brewed tamari) — no harm to male
  hormones or a normal thyroid; only avoid ultra-processed soy protein isolate. Coconut aminos is a
  clean 1:1 soy-free swap for cooking.
- **[CONC] The hidden cost is spoilage, not shelf price.** Buy frozen off-season (equal-or-better
  nutrition than shipped "fresh"), batch-cook a grain + protein + eggs weekly, freeze portions. A
  clean high-protein week lands **~$85–100/week** vs. **$25–40/day** for the same nutrition as
  bars/shakes/deli/meal-kits. Prices drift weekly — calibrate on your first shop.
