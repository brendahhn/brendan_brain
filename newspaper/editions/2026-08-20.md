## Health

**This week's evidence review: the gut–heart link — and why it's more reassuring than scary.** A careful pass connecting two things usually studied separately (inflammatory bowel disease and heart-disease risk). General evidence, not medical advice.

- **[A] The reassuring headline: IBD's extra heart risk is *activity-dependent*.** Yes, IBD carries a modestly higher lifetime cardiovascular risk (~20% higher relative risk in pooled studies) — but a large nationwide cohort shows that excess is concentrated in **flares and active disease**, and during **remission the risk of heart attack/stroke is about the same as the general population**. Keeping the gut disease well-controlled is doubly worth it: it protects the heart too.
- **[B] The mechanism is intuitive.** A chronically inflamed, "leakier" gut lets bacterial fragments trickle into the bloodstream, feeding low-grade inflammation that contributes to artery disease over decades — the same barrier/inflammation story that drives flares, now with a cardiovascular tail. So the everyday levers that calm gut inflammation (staying in remission, a high-fiber/fermented plant-forward diet, good sleep, stress down-regulation) plausibly do quiet cardiovascular good as well. And there's now hard proof that inflammation *itself* — not just cholesterol — drives heart events (a major trial cut repeat cardiac events by blocking one inflammatory signal, independent of cholesterol).
- **[B] De-hype worth knowing generally:** this does **not** mean buying a "TMAO test" or a gut-health-for-your-heart supplement. The gut-metabolite (TMAO) and short-chain-fatty-acid stories are real but soft — TMAO is largely lowered by simply eating less red meat and more plants. The proven levers stay boring: control inflammation, and the classic basics (cholesterol/ApoB, blood pressure, fitness, not smoking, sleep). _(General evidence, not medical advice; detail kept in the health repo.)_
