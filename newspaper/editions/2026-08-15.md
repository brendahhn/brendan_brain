## Health

### The body-clock lens on migraine
<sub>source: [`from-health-robot.md`](queue/inbox/from-health-robot.md) (run 2026-08-14) · [`knowledge-20260814-migraine-clock-timed-fixed-wake`](domains/health/knowledge/knowledge-20260814-migraine-clock-timed-fixed-wake.md)</sub>

- **[CONC, tier A]** Migraine is partly governed by the internal clock — the largest analysis to date found ~half of sufferers have a daily rhythm to their attacks, genetics pointing at the brain's master clock. A steady schedule is a mechanism-matched lever.
- **[CONC, tier A]** The single highest-value move is a **fixed wake time** (same time daily, minimal weekend drift). It's the same habit that steadies the gut's daily rhythm — one free, no-side-effect behavior, two systems.
- **[OBS, tier B]** Attacks cluster in the early-morning hours; sufferers tend to have a rigid body clock, which is why **irregular** sleep (especially weekend catch-up) provokes more than merely short sleep. The fix is regularity, not more hours.
- **[OBS, tier B]** Melatonin runs low in migraine and has real preventive evidence (beats placebo in most trials), but whether/when to add it is a **prescriber call** where evening timing matters. Clean interaction-wise, still a clinician decision.
- **[ASSUME]** Honesty check: no trial has directly tested "regular schedule → fewer migraines," so the dual-lever idea is a mechanism-based bet to track yourself; a "night-shift work causes migraine" analysis is still only a preprint — noted, not banked.
