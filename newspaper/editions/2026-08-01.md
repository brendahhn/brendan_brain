### [CONC] Antiperspirant is safe at the dose your skin actually absorbs — and "natural" deodorant fails by design, not by defect
<sub>source: [`from-health-robot.md`](queue/inbox/from-health-robot.md)</sub>

This directly answers your 07-23 question ("is antiperspirant bad for me, and why do I smell by noon?") — one of your open batch tasks.

- **[A]** **Antiperspirant aluminum barely crosses skin** — a radiotracer study measured **~0.012% absorption (~4 µg per application)**, a rounding error next to the aluminum in a normal day's food. The "it builds up and poisons you" premise fails at the dose.
- **[A]** **No breast-cancer link** — a 2024 meta-analysis of 7 case-control studies found no association (OR 0.96).
- **[A]** **Deodorant only masks odor; antiperspirant (aluminum) is the only thing that stops sweat.** So an aluminum-free "natural" stick losing by midday is it **working as designed**, not failing. The evidence-based fix if you want to stay dry: use a **real antiperspirant applied at NIGHT on dry skin** — the plug forms overnight and holds through the next day, beating a morning swipe.
- **[B]** The **Alzheimer's** worry targets the wrong route: a 2025 meta of 54 studies found ~30% higher risk only with **high environmental/occupational** aluminum (water, dust, old dialysis medicine) — ingested/inhaled and observational, not the trivial dermal dose from a stick. The FDA "ask a doctor if you have kidney disease" label applies only to **advanced (stage 4–5)** kidney disease.
- **[B]** **Chapstick:** pick a plain **fragrance-free balm with mineral SPF**; avoid nut-derived oils and classic contact-allergens (propolis, lanolin, peppermint/flavor, fragrance). Carrageenan in balm is a non-issue (trace, not a documented lip allergen).
