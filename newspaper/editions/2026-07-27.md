### [CONC] Run 35 — you feed a gut microbiome, you don't buy one
<sub>source: [`from-health-robot.md`](queue/inbox/from-health-robot.md) · [`knowledge`](domains/health/knowledge/knowledge-20260726-microbiome-resilience-is-an-output.md)</sub>

The gut-microbiome/SIBO chapter's throughline: microbiome resilience is an **output** of diet and behavior, not an input you can dose.

- **[CONC-B] Spend on food, not kits.** The protective "keystone" gut bacteria are oxygen-sensitive or have no viable product — they're cultivated by a diverse, fiber-rich diet (a rotation of fast/medium/slow-fermenting fibers plus psyllium), not swallowed as capsules. Most of the achievable benefit is free.
- **[CONC-B] Skip the direct-to-consumer microbiome tests** (GI-MAP, Viome). They have no established clinical validity — the same stool sample sent to different labs returns different answers. For gut inflammation the validated test is **fecal calprotectin**.
- **[CONC-A] SIBO is genuinely more common in IBD** (~1-in-4 to 1-in-3), but the breath test is contested and overgrowth may be a *consequence* rather than a driver — gate testing to persistent symptoms, not curiosity, and use antibiotics sparingly.
- **[CONC-B] Fermented foods** (yogurt, kefir, kraut, kimchi) are a cheap diversity add — but aged/fermented items run high in histamine, a caveat for histamine or oral-allergy sensitivity.

This chapter's strongest finding — a probiotic strain matching a standard maintenance drug in trials — is a **clinician conversation, never a self-directed swap** (the standing provider-gate rule). Robot's general-science conclusions; not yet Brendan-confirmed.
