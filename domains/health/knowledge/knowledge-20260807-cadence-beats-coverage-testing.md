The highest-value health-admin move for a young, healthy person in a well-managed chronic
condition is to pick a small set of validated readouts and put them on a fixed recurring
schedule, rather than chasing a wide panel. **Cadence beats coverage.** At a young, well-managed
baseline the real failure mode is over-testing into anxiety and false positives, not
under-testing: a lean calendar (a few high-signal recurring tests + free wearable/physical
self-tests) beats a big annual "everything" panel and expensive multi-cancer/microbiome kits,
which are low-yield at that age.

Two structuring insights make it actually happen:

1. **Separate the MENU from the CALENDAR.** The testing menu (what to test, targets, cost) is a
   different artifact from the scheduling layer (when each thing hits the calendar, anchored to
   today plus a few personal dates). Most "get these tests" advice stops at the menu; the
   calendar is what turns intent into action.

2. **Keep two clocks.** A medical/testing cadence and a training/body-composition cadence run in
   parallel. The body clock's single feedback signal is a weekly weigh-in average (adjust intake
   up or down off the trend), with a maintenance-effort fallback for busy/high-stress weeks so the
   routine never becomes a stressor, and a fixed re-test interval for fitness markers.
