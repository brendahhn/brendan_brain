**Core finding (reassuring):**
- **[A] IBD carries a modestly higher lifetime cardiovascular risk** (ischemic-heart-disease relative
  risk ~1.2; ulcerative colitis ~1.21 — Feng, *J Am Heart Assoc* 2017) — **but that excess is
  activity-dependent.** A Danish nationwide cohort (Kristensen, *PLoS One* 2013, PMID 23457642) found
  heart-attack/stroke/CV-death risk elevated during flares and persistent activity, and **normalized to
  the general population during remission.** Practical read: keeping the gut disease well-controlled is
  doubly worth it — remission is, quietly, also heart protection.

**Mechanism (intuitive, same barrier/inflammation story as flares):**
- **[B]** A chronically inflamed, "leakier" gut lets bacterial fragments (LPS→TLR4) trickle into the
  bloodstream, feeding low-grade endotoxemia and vascular inflammation/atherothrombosis over decades
  (Violi, *Nat Rev Cardiol* 2023). The everyday levers that calm gut inflammation — staying in
  remission, a high-fiber/fermented plant-forward diet, good sleep, stress down-regulation — plausibly
  do quiet cardiovascular good as well.
- **[B]** Hard proof that inflammation *itself* (not just cholesterol) drives heart events comes from
  CANTOS (Ridker, *NEJM* 2017, PMID 28845751): blocking one inflammatory signal cut repeat cardiac
  events independent of cholesterol. Used here as an **evidence anchor, not a prevention drug** — the
  agent raised serious-infection risk, so the everyday translation is "lower the inflammation you
  already have a handle on."

**Belief-corrections / de-hype worth knowing generally:**
- **[B]** This does **not** justify buying a "TMAO test" or a gut-health-for-your-heart supplement. The
  gut-metabolite (TMAO) and short-chain-fatty-acid stories are real but soft: TMAO is largely lowered by
  simply eating less red meat and more plants, and fiber-metabolite blood-pressure benefits are mostly
  animal-grade so far.
- The proven levers stay boring: control inflammation, and the classic basics — cholesterol/ApoB, Lp(a),
  blood pressure, fitness, not smoking, sleep.
