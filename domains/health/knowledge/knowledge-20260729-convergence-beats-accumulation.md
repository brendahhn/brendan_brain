When building any system of habits or interventions, rank the candidates by how many separate
goals each one serves and invest in the few multi-purpose levers rather than a long list of
single-purpose ones. Convergence beats accumulation. This is the central finding of the
health project's 36-chapter master synthesis (Run 38, final chapter): a handful of "different"
health habits turn out to be ONE habit wearing many hats — sleep, an aerobic base (Zone 2 +
one weekly hard interval), strength training with adequate protein, and a few minutes of daily
slow breathing each pay off across six-plus health domains at once, so doing those four well
captures the large majority of everything else. The principle is explicitly cross-domain: in
any system (finances, tooling, career skills, a research program), a small set of levers that
each cut across many objectives outperforms a sprawling checklist of one-off tactics. Two
corollaries from the same synthesis carry the same shape: "behavior over bottle" (default to a
free behavioral lever before a purchased product when both target the same goal) and "decision
literacy is the real deliverable" (the durable win from a research project is a repeatable
method to evaluate the NEXT claim yourself, not just the current answer). Flagged by
health-robot (Run 38, master synthesis). Not yet Brendan-confirmed.
