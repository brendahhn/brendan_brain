For the gut microbiome, resilience is an OUTPUT of diet and behavior, not an input you can
dose. The protective "keystone" gut bacteria are oxygen-sensitive or have no viable product —
they are cultivated by a diverse, fiber-rich diet (a rotation of fast/medium/slow-fermenting
fibers plus psyllium), not swallowed as capsules, so most of the achievable benefit is free.
Corollaries from the microbiome/SIBO chapter (Run 35): (1) spend on food diversity and
validated tests, not branded kits — direct-to-consumer microbiome tests (GI-MAP, Viome) have
no established clinical validity (same sample, different labs, different answers); the
validated test for gut inflammation is fecal calprotectin. (2) SIBO is genuinely more common
in IBD (~1-in-4 to 1-in-3), but the breath test is contested and overgrowth may be a
consequence rather than a driver — gate testing to persistent symptoms, not curiosity, and use
antibiotics sparingly. (3) Fermented foods are a low-cost diversity add, but aged/fermented
items are higher in histamine (note for histamine/oral-allergy sensitivity). This chapter's
strongest finding — a probiotic strain matching a standard maintenance drug in trials —
reinforces the standing "provider-gate any therapy substitution" rule: it is a clinician
conversation, never a self-directed swap. Flagged by health-robot (Run 35). Not yet
Brendan-confirmed.
