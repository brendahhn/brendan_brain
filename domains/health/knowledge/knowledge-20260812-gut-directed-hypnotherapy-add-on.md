Health literacy that generalizes, from a directly-requested deep dive (basis of the Nerva app).

1. **It's a standardized medical technique, not stage hypnosis.** Gut-directed hypnotherapy is
   the most-researched non-drug treatment for irritable bowel syndrome — ~70-80% of people
   improve in the classic in-person trials, with benefit lasting years, and major GI guidelines
   list it as a legitimate option. It works by calming an over-sensitive gut-brain signaling
   loop (a "top-down" retraining of how the brain reads gut signals).

2. **Its standout result is in inflammatory bowel disease, with a hard caveat.** One randomized
   trial found that adding gut-directed hypnotherapy helped people with quiescent ulcerative
   colitis stay in remission longer than a control group over a year — it's the only therapy of
   its class with that kind of result. Framing that matters: it appears to delay flares / ease
   symptoms via the stress-gut pathway; it is NOT anti-inflammatory the way a medication is. It
   is strictly an ADD-ON, never a reason to reduce medication.

3. **Honesty check on the apps.** The single largest trial of an app-delivered version (a
   prescription app, not Nerva) missed its primary goal, and Nerva itself has no controlled
   trial — only a real-world audit where most people didn't finish and the ones who did tended
   to improve. Realistic expectation: "a meaningful benefit IF you complete the ~6-week
   program," not a guaranteed win. Adherence is the make-or-break variable.

4. **How it fits the other calm-the-body tools.** Hypnotherapy ("top-down") pairs with
   "bottom-up" tools like slow breathing — they complement rather than compete (connects to the
   vagus/cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway, knowledge-20260811). Among gut-brain options,
   hypnotherapy is the one that is both safe-to-self-start AND has direct colitis-remission
   evidence; a nerve-stimulation device has colitis evidence but needs a clinician's sign-off,
   and breathing is safe but unproven specifically for the gut.
