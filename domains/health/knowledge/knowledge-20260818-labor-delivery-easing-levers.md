**The ranked, evidence-based levers (highest evidence first):**
1. **[A] Continuous one-to-one support during labor** is the best-evidenced no-cost, no-drug lever.
   A large Cochrane review (Bohren 2017, PMID 28681500 — 27 trials, ~15,800 women) found it raises
   the chance of a spontaneous vaginal birth and lowers cesarean, instrumental delivery, and epidural
   use, with shorter labor and no harms. The effect is **largest when the continuous supporter is a
   trained doula** (vs hospital staff or a family member); a present partner still adds real value and
   stacks on top.
2. **[A] Upright / mobile positions in early (first-stage) labor** — walking, birth ball — shorten
   the first stage by roughly 1.4 hours vs lying down (Lawrence 2013, PMID 24105444).
3. **[A] Regular pregnancy exercise** (once medically cleared) lowers the odds of an instrumental
   delivery with no added harm (Davenport 2019, PMID 30337349).

**Prep levers with solid but narrower evidence:**
- **[B] Antenatal perineal massage** from ~34–35 weeks reduces tearing and episiotomy, mainly for a
  first baby (Beckmann 2013, PMID 23633325).
- **[B] Second-stage warm compresses / perineal support** during pushing may reduce severe tears
  (Aasheim 2017).
- **[B] First-stage water immersion** lowers epidural use (Cluett 2018, PMID 29768662).

**Belief-corrections worth knowing generally:**
- **"Natural induction" supplements do not hold up.** Evening primrose oil and raspberry-leaf tea
  show no cervical-ripening benefit in the trials (EPO carries some safety flags).
- **Eating dates late in pregnancy** modestly improves cervical readiness but does **not** reduce
  cesarean rates (tier C).
- **Routine continuous fetal monitoring (CTG) in a genuinely low-risk labor** raises the cesarean
  rate (RR ~1.66) without improving the baby's survival vs intermittent listening — worth knowing when
  building a birth plan.
