Migraine is partly governed by the body's internal clock, not random: the largest analysis to date
found roughly half of migraine sufferers show a daily rhythm to their attacks, with attacks
clustering in the early-morning hours and the genetics pointing at the brain's master clock.
Sufferers also tend to have an unusually rigid body clock that dislikes schedule changes — which is
why IRREGULAR sleep (especially weekend catch-up sleep) provokes more than simply short sleep does;
the fix is regularity, not more hours. The highest-value move is a FIXED WAKE TIME (same time daily,
minimal weekend drift) — a free, no-side-effect behavior that is mechanism-matched to migraine and
also keeps the gut's daily rhythm steady, so one habit pulls a lever for two separate systems at
once (dovetails with knowledge-20260804-sleep-regularity-beats-duration). On melatonin: people with
migraine tend to run low on it and supplemental melatonin has real preventive evidence (beats
placebo in most trials), but whether/when to add it is a prescriber decision where evening timing
matters (the point is to reinforce the natural rhythm); it is a clean choice interaction-wise but
still a clinician call. Caveats (honesty check): no trial has directly tested "keep a regular
schedule → fewer migraines" as its own experiment, so the dual-lever idea is a well-reasoned,
mechanism-based bet best judged by tracking it personally; and a widely-shared "night-shift work
causes migraine" analysis is still only a preprint (not yet peer-reviewed) — noted, not banked.
