Health literacy that generalizes, from a mechanism-level research pass. This puts a named
circuit under the earlier "controls inflammation ≠ manages the stress response" principle
(knowledge-20260808): the mechanism is no longer hand-wavy.

1. **There is a real, well-established nerve pathway.** The vagus nerve's *cholinergic
   anti-inflammatory pathway* releases a signal that tells immune cells to dial down inflammatory
   chemicals. Slow breathing, heart-rate-variability practice, aerobic training, and regular
   sleep all raise the tone of this same pathway. Those free habits are hitting a genuine
   mechanism — a strong reason to keep them non-negotiable rather than treat them as nice-to-haves.

2. **A drug and this pathway are different biological arms.** A medication can control
   inflammation and still not really reach this nerve-driven "calm-the-immune-system" arm —
   which is exactly why the behavioral habits keep earning their place. Confirming the mechanism
   is a reason to invest in the habits, not to shop for a new product.

3. **"Targets a real mechanism" still isn't "safe/proven for me."** There is a real, non-invasive
   device — a small ear-clip that stimulates a branch of the vagus nerve — with early
   proof-of-concept evidence of lowering a gut-inflammation marker, including in some
   inflammatory-bowel patients. Genuinely promising, but the evidence is early, and any
   nerve-stimulation device is a clinician conversation — doubly so alongside any
   heart-rate-affecting medication. It is NOT a self-start purchase.

4. **Mechanism-plausible ≠ safe (the nicotine trap).** The receptor this pathway uses happens to
   be one nicotine also binds. That does NOT make nicotine/vaping a "gut hack" — that's a
   separate, hazardous question this pass did not evaluate. Mechanism-plausible ≠ safe or proven.
