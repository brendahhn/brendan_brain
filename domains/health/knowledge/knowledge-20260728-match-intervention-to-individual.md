When a wellness practice has a real mechanism, check whether the benefit and the cost point
the same way for THIS person before adopting it — the same intervention can be a net win for a
general athlete and net-negative for someone whose condition runs on the stress axis (e.g.
deliberate cold is an acute stress activator). Match the intervention to the individual, not to
the trend. This is a cross-domain principle: it generalizes to any tool whose mechanism cuts
both ways depending on the user's baseline. From the cold/heat exposure chapter (Run 37): (1)
heat has the better-evidenced, cheaper route — 8 weeks of regular hot baths improved
vascular function/arterial stiffness/BP about as much as exercise training in a controlled
study, whereas the sauna-mortality data are observational; a hot bath beats chasing a sauna.
(2) Don't ice-bath right after lifting — post-workout cold-water immersion measurably blunts
muscle/strength gains over 12 weeks because it suppresses the inflammatory growth signal; put
cold on rest days or after skill/cardio, not after strength work. (3) Cold plunges are a
legit stress/sleep tool (best pooled evidence: ~3,000 people, lower stress and better sleep)
but NOT a proven mood or immune booster, and they raise inflammation short-term. (4) "Finish on
cold for brown fat / metabolism" and grounding/earthing mats and infrared blankets are
unproven or sales-driven — skip them. Reinforces the standing provider-gate rule (this chapter
changed no dose, med, or supplement; its only clinical flag is a cold-exposure × heart-rate-
medication caution — "autonomic conflict" risk, provider-gated and health-domain-scoped).
