A brewed beverage and its concentrated extract capsule can be pharmacologically different
animals, and the difference is dose. The worked example is green tea: a brewed cup sits far
below the threshold where green-tea EXTRACT starts inhibiting drug-metabolizing liver enzymes
(CYP) and carrying an idiosyncratic liver-injury signal (~≥800 mg EGCG/day per EFSA). The same
gap shows up on the benefit side — the placebo-controlled anti-inflammatory signal in
ulcerative colitis was at EXTRACT doses, not a cup of tea, so the drink is a mild bonus, not a
treatment. Operational rule: default to the food/beverage form and enjoy it freely; treat a
concentrated botanical extract as a drug — a "run it past your doctor" item, especially on
regular medication. Rhymes with "base beats specialty" and "provider-gate supplement changes,"
and extends the consumer-literacy line that the LABEL/form beats the marketing. Not yet
Brendan-confirmed.
