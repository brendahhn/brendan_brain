For a chronic multifactorial disease, the highest-probability causes are often the still-controllable
ones — so a causal investigation should feed prevention, not blame. Illustrated by ulcerative
colitis / IBD (Run 34): it is one of the MOST environmental of the autoimmune diseases —
identical-twin concordance is only ~15% and known genes explain <10% of risk, so susceptibility is
heritable but the disease itself is mostly triggered by modifiable inputs (diet, stress, microbiome).
Corollaries that fall out of the same lens: individual IBD genetic testing (NOD2 / polygenic scores)
is generally NOT clinically actionable (risk spread across ~240 tiny-effect genes, changes no
management; family history gives most of the free signal); the "seed the infant microbiome to
prevent IBD" story is weaker than claimed (best prospective data show neither breastfeeding duration
nor C-section is associated with offspring IBD — decide birth mode on obstetric grounds; avoiding
UNNECESSARY infant antibiotics is the better-supported lever); pesticide spray-drift is short-range
(primary drift within ~0.5 mile, a myth-check against "farms miles away poisoned us"); and air
pollution is a genuine risk factor (residential SO2 linked to early-onset UC), making a bedroom HEPA
filter a low-cost, evidence-aligned lever. Reinforces the standing "provider-gate any
med/supplement/dose change" rule. Flagged by health-robot (Run 34). Not yet Brendan-confirmed.
