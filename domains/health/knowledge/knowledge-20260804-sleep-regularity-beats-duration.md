The strongest sleep lever is the CONSISTENCY of the schedule, not squeezing out more hours. In a
60,000+ person study using wearable data, people with the most regular sleep/wake timing had
20–48% lower all-cause mortality than the most irregular — and regularity out-predicted total
hours slept. The practical move is a fixed wake-up time seven days a week (including weekends),
shifting the whole window earlier on early days rather than "catching up" later. Two related
findings from the same cross-examination: (1) in a meta-analysis of 72 studies it was sleep
DISTURBANCE (fragmented sleep) and LONG sleep — not short sleep on its own — that tracked with
higher inflammation, so the goal is to protect continuity and NOT chase 9+ hours or "bank" sleep
with weekend lie-ins; and (2) insomnia is a 24-hour stress-system disorder ("tired but wired,"
elevated stress-hormone output peaking in the evening/first half of night), so a racing-mind night
is best treated by winding the nervous system down (slow breathing, cyclic sighing, CBT-I if
chronic) rather than reaching for a sedative. Sleep, the body clock, and the gut appear to run as
one connected system — more diverse gut microbiomes correlate with better sleep quality in people,
though the gut-barrier mechanism is animal-level so far. Cross-domain takeaway: fix the schedule
before chasing more hours. Flagged by health-robot (08-04 sleep cross-examination). Not yet
Brendan-confirmed.
