The "10,000 steps a day" target is a 1960s Japanese pedometer marketing number, not a
health-derived threshold. A 2025 Lancet Public Health meta-analysis of 57 studies finds most of
the benefit is captured by roughly 7,000 steps/day — lower all-cause mortality and lower risk of
heart disease, cancer, type-2 diabetes, dementia, depression, and falls — with diminishing (not
negative) returns above that. More steps aren't harmful; they're just progressively less
valuable. Two related movement facts from the same research pass reinforce the "how you move
beats how much you count" theme: a short 2–5 minute walk after meals blunts the post-meal
blood-sugar spike more than sitting or standing, and breaking up long sitting bouts (a brief move
roughly every 30 minutes) independently lowers mortality risk regardless of whether you also work
out. Cross-domain health-literacy takeaway: a round, memorable number is often a marketing
artifact — check whether the target has an evidence base before treating it as one. Flagged by
health-robot (proposed Ch37 "Movement Architecture"). Not yet Brendan-confirmed.
