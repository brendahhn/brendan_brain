Evidence layer from health-robot Run (2026-08-16): search for a **safe** supplement acting on
the specific stress→CRH→mast-cell→gut-permeability pathway that common IBD/autoimmune meds
(S1P-modulators, 5-ASA) tend to leave uncovered. Confidence **low** — the mechanism is plausible
and the safety-on-paper argument is sound, but the human efficacy evidence does not match Brendan's
actual condition.

**The candidate: PEA (palmitoylethanolamide).** An endogenous compound (the body makes it itself)
that down-regulates mast-cell activation. Its distinguishing advantage over the usual "gut/stress"
botanicals — berberine, curcumin, quercetin — is the **clearance route**: PEA is broken down by
FAAH/NAAA, **not** the CYP2C8/2C9/3A4 liver enzymes those drugs depend on. So on paper it does not
compete for the same metabolic pathway, which is exactly the collision that makes most botanicals a
bad idea alongside CYP-cleared medication.

**The honest caveat that keeps this out of "self-start" territory:**
- The good human trials for PEA are in **IBS (irritable bowel), not ulcerative colitis**, and used
  **combination products** — so efficacy for Brendan's condition is unproven, and this is a "raise
  it with your GI as a possible symptom-support add-on," never a reason to change existing meds.
- **Buyer-beware, general rule:** the popular **"PEA + luteolin"** combos are a *worse* choice for
  anyone on a CYP-cleared drug — luteolin **inhibits CYP2C9**, reintroducing the very interaction
  PEA-alone avoids. If PEA is ever tried, plain **ultramicronized PEA alone** is the cleaner form.

**Where it sits in the stack (reinforced this week):** for the stress→gut pathway these meds miss,
the safest and best-evidenced levers remain **free and behavioral** — consistent sleep/wake timing,
a daily slow-breathing block, gut-directed hypnotherapy. Supplements sit *below* them on both
evidence and cost; PEA is a below-the-line "worth asking a doctor about," not a lever to pull now.

Not medical advice; any supplement-alongside-medication decision is a prescriber's.
