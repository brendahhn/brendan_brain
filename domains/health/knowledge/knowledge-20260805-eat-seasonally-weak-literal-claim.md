Eat-with-the-seasons is a sensible organizing habit built on a weak literal claim: no trial shows
seasonal eating improves any health outcome (the best review calls the evidence "scant"), so it's a
habit, not a treatment, and shouldn't be sold as one. Its real payoffs are indirect. (1) FRESHNESS /
short supply chains: produce loses vitamins fast after harvest (packaged spinach lost ~half its
folate within a week in the fridge), so "local + in-season" wins by shortening the field-to-plate
clock, not because the month is special. (2) A belief-correction worth banking: for out-of-season
produce, FROZEN is nutritionally equal to — often better than — "fresh" that's been shipped and then
sat for days, because flash-freezing locks in nutrients within hours of harvest; buying frozen
off-season is the higher-nutrient, lower-cost choice, not a compromise. (3) PLANT VARIETY: eating
seasonally forces rotation through different plants across the year, and diversity (not the season)
is what feeds a broader microbiome — dramatic seasonal microbiome "cycling" is documented in
hunter-gatherers but muted by year-round supermarket availability. Practical rule: buy local+fresh
when it's in season and cheap; for everything out-of-season, buy frozen. Flagged by health-robot
(08-05 "eat with the seasons" research). Not yet Brendan-confirmed.
