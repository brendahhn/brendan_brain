The cheapest sources of protein-per-dollar — dried beans/lentils, eggs, whole tofu, canned wild
salmon, rotisserie/thigh chicken, plain Greek yogurt — are also the least processed and
additive-free. The expensive convenience proteins (bars, ready-to-drink shakes, deli meats) are
simultaneously the worst value per gram of protein AND where the emulsifiers/fillers hide, so
swapping bars/deli for eggs + a yogurt-or-tofu base + canned fish cuts cost ~50–70% per gram of
protein and removes the additives in one move. Clean and cheap point at the same grocery list;
you don't have to trade one for the other.

Two supporting corrections worth banking: (1) SOY is fine and worth it as WHOLE soy — tofu,
tempeh, edamame, naturally-brewed tamari are clean, cheap protein that don't harm male hormones
or a normal thyroid; the only soy worth avoiding is ultra-processed soy protein isolate in
bars/shakes (coconut aminos is a clean 1:1 soy-free swap for cooking). (2) The hidden cost that
turns "cheap eating" expensive is SPOILAGE, not shelf price — buying protein/berries/veg frozen
(nutritionally equal-or-better than shipped "fresh"), batch-cooking a grain + a protein + eggs
weekly, and freezing portions drives food waste toward zero, which is what actually makes a low
per-day cost real. Safe-handling backbone: cool cooked food within 2 hours, fridge 3–4 days max,
freeze beyond that. A clean, high-protein, high-fiber week built this way lands ~$85–100/week vs.
$25–40/day for the same nutrition bought as bars, shakes, deli, and meal kits.
