The intuition that "bad posture is wrecking my back/neck" is largely a myth. An umbrella review of
41 systematic reviews finds no consensus that resting posture causes spinal pain, and "text neck"
studies show no reliable association between neck flexion and pain. The better-supported anti-pain
principle is to move often and vary position — the harm is in holding ONE fixed position for a
long time, not in which position it is — rather than chasing a single "correct" posture. (Posture
still earns its keep for appearance and confidence, just not as a pain-prevention target.) This
pairs with the extensor-endurance finding from the same research pass: back durability tracks with
trained endurance of the spinal extensors and with movement variety, not with sitting "up
straight." Cross-domain health-literacy takeaway: a widely-repeated cause-and-effect story
("posture → pain") can survive for decades on plausibility alone while the systematic-review
evidence never actually supported it. Flagged by health-robot (proposed Ch37 "Movement
Architecture"). Not yet Brendan-confirmed.
