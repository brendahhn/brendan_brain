Two health-literacy principles that generalize beyond any one condition, from a mechanism-level
research pass:

1. **"Controls inflammation" and "manages the stress→barrier response" are different jobs.** A
   drug can do the first and leave the second uncovered — they are different biological arms. For
   the stress-driven arm there is often no good pill, which is exactly why the durable lever there
   is behavioral: consistent breathing practice (cyclic sighing), regular sleep timing, and
   acceptance/reappraisal skills. Confirming this at the mechanism level is a reason to keep
   investing in the free habits, not to go shopping for a new product.

2. **Mechanism-plausible ≠ proven.** Self-prescribing antihistamines or "mast-cell
   stabilizer"/anti-allergy supplements for gut symptoms is mechanism-plausible and an active
   research area — but it is NOT proven therapy. That's a clinician conversation, not a
   self-experiment.
