The cheapest and most portable stress tool is your own breath, and the mechanism is specific and
directional: a slow, complete exhale that is longer than the inhale (roughly a 1:2 ratio) is what
triggers the calming (parasympathetic) response. Long exhale = calm; fast inhale = alert. The
best-evidenced quick practice is "cyclic sighing" — a double inhale (a normal breath through the
nose, then a short sip on top) followed by a long, slow exhale through the mouth; a 2023 Stanford
randomized trial found 5 minutes a day for a month improved mood and lowered breathing rate more
than box breathing, "power/Wim-Hof-style" hyperventilation, or mindfulness meditation (effect sizes
modest, volunteers healthy). A single physiological sigh (1–3 breaths) is the fastest real-time
reset for an acute stress or anger spike. The direction matters and is an easy mistake:
inhale-emphasized rapid breathing revs you UP, so for stress reduction the long-exhale version is
the tool, not the hyperventilation version. Cross-domain: this is a deliberate lever to reach for
before any high-stakes moment — an interview, a hard conversation, a trade decision. Flagged by
