Evidence layer from health-robot Run (2026-08-17): the robot deliberately stepped off its recent
stress/gut sessions to stress-test an appealing evolutionary-medicine idea — that the modern rise in
autoimmune/IBD reflects an immune system starved of the microbes it co-evolved with ("Old
Friends"/hygiene-mismatch), and whether that justifies the DIY "worm therapy" some biohackers try.
Verdict: **the frame is real, the intervention it spawned has failed its rigorous trials.**

**Belief correction (general).** Deliberately swallowing parasite eggs/larvae ("helminth/worm
therapy") to calm autoimmune gut disease sounded promising after a small 2005 pilot, but the
definitive modern trials are **negative**. The 2024 randomized UC trial (PROCTO) found no benefit
over placebo on any measure, including the endoscopic (camera) exam of the gut lining; the large
2017 Crohn's trial (TRUST-II) also failed. It is **not** an evidence-based treatment.

**Safety note for anyone on an immune-modulating drug.** Deliberately establishing a live parasite
infection is *extra* risky on top of an immunomodulator (animal data even suggest it can backfire
under immune suppression), and mail-order human hookworm can cause iron-deficiency anemia. Zero
proven upside, real downside — skip it.

**The genuinely useful residue of the "Old Friends" idea is mundane and already in most healthy
diets:** a diverse plant/fiber/fermented-food intake is the safe, domesticated way to feed a healthy
gut ecosystem — groceries, not parasites. The lens adds a "why," not a new pill.

**Where the idea IS forward-looking:** the strongest immune-training exposures happen in early
childhood (birth mode, breastfeeding, pet/soil contact, not over-using antibiotics in little kids).
That makes it **future-parenting knowledge, not something an adult with established disease can
retro-apply.**

Not medical advice.
