From a directly-requested deep dive that consolidated a pile of loose "stress tips" into an
evidence-ranked, real-time toolkit. Organizing rule: **use the body to steady the mind first,
then the reframe** — "body before story."

1. **Fastest reliable in-the-moment tool is a specific breath, not a thought.** A "double-inhale
   then long exhale" (the *physiological sigh*) beat other breathing styles and meditation
   head-to-head for calming mood in a randomized trial. Do it FIRST when you feel activated. This
   extends the earlier long-exhale finding (knowledge-20260730): the double-inhale/long-exhale is
   the specific, best-supported form.

2. **Reframe nerves as excitement — but stack it, don't lead with it.** Telling yourself the
   racing heart is your body getting you ready (rather than forcing calm) produced a healthier
   cardiovascular stress response and better performance across several trials. Sequence:
   **breathe first, then reframe.**

3. **"Stress-is-enhancing" is real but small.** Viewing stress as enhancing rather than harmful
   is a trainable belief that nudges stress-hormone response toward a healthier profile — but the
   overall effect of reframe/mindset techniques is SMALL in a 2024 meta-analysis (effect ~0.2).
   Treat it as a pre-installed edge, not a rescue button.

4. **Correction #1 — the eye-movement trick is mis-stated.** The "move your eyes side-to-side to
   switch off fear before public speaking" idea is wrong as usually told. That EMDR-style
   technique reduces the *vividness of a distressing mental image you're recalling* (best done
   beforehand, on the specific worry) — it is NOT an in-the-moment amygdala off-switch.

5. **Correction #2 — power-breathing is a threshold practice, not a calm-down.** Intense
   Wim-Hof-style breathing (rapid breaths + breath-holds) is a big *adrenaline surge*, a
   threshold-builder, not a calming tool; its striking anti-inflammation result comes from a
   healthy-volunteer lab model, not any gut-disease study. **Safety flag:** breath-holds carry a
   cardiac caution for anyone on a heart-rate-lowering medication — gentle slow breathing gives
   the calming benefit without the risk; a doctor conversation is warranted before the intense
   version.

6. **Higher stress threshold via exercise/exposure is plausible but mixed.** A reasonable bonus of
   a cardio habit, not a guaranteed effect.
