Deodorant vs. antiperspirant is a real distinction, not marketing: deodorant only masks odor, while
antiperspirant (aluminum) is the only thing that actually stops sweat. So an aluminum-free "natural"
deodorant losing the battle by midday is the product working as designed, not failing — the
evidence-based fix if you want to stay dry is a real antiperspirant applied at NIGHT on dry skin (the
plug forms overnight and holds through the next day, working better than a morning swipe). The
aluminum-scare premises don't survive the dose that skin actually absorbs: a radiotracer study
measured ~0.012% dermal absorption (~4 micrograms per application), a rounding error next to the
aluminum in a normal day's food; a 2024 meta-analysis of 7 case-control studies found no
antiperspirant/breast-cancer association (OR 0.96); and the Alzheimer's worry is aimed at the wrong
route — a 2025 meta of 54 studies found ~30% higher risk only with HIGH environmental/occupational
aluminum (water, dust, old dialysis medicine), which is ingested/inhaled and observational, not the
trivial dermal dose from a stick. General consumer-product literacy. Flagged by health-robot; not yet
Brendan-confirmed.
