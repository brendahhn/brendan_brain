Measure the trend, not the snapshot — and don't over-test into anxiety; for a healthy young
person the real failure mode is false positives, not under-testing. This is a cross-domain
principle: it applies to any KPI or dashboard (jobs pipeline, trading book) as much as to
health markers. Corollaries from the biomarkers/testing synthesis chapter (Run 36): (1)
inflammation-sensitive labs must never be read in isolation — ferritin, hs-CRP, and similar
acute-phase reactants are distorted by any active inflammatory state, so pair them with
other inflammation markers (e.g. ferritin with transferrin saturation) before drawing a
conclusion. (2) Match the marker to the person: cystatin C is a cleaner kidney readout than
creatinine for someone actively building muscle; the omega-3 index (target >8%) is one cheap
test that tells you whether fish oil is actually working. (3) Testing economics — a
provider-ordered core panel (often insurance-covered) plus ONE à-la-carte "missing-markers"
panel delivers ~95% of a premium subscription's value at roughly a third of the cost;
subscriptions buy a dashboard, not better data. (4) Multi-cancer early-detection blood tests
(e.g. Galleri, ~$949) are NOT worth it for a healthy person under ~50 — validated only in
older/higher-risk groups, not FDA/USPSTF-endorsed, and at a low base rate a "positive" is
mostly a false alarm that triggers needless invasive workups. Reinforces the standing
provider-gate rule (this chapter changed no dose, med, or supplement). Flagged by
